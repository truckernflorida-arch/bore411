Where Florida bore work gets posted

The official places the fourteen counties around Arcadia, their main cities, FDOT and the regional water buyers post bids for directional boring, force mains, water mains, conduit and other underground work. Each card says which platform the agency uses and what its own page says about signing up. Links checked November 28, 2026. The Bore411 lead board lists bore jobs from agencies like these every morning; this page is for checking the source yourself and getting the agency's own notices.

Official agency pages only. Bore411 is not affiliated with any agency or bid platform and does not bid work, take a percentage of any job, or hold or share contractor licenses. Platforms and rules change; the solicitation documents and addenda govern. Some platforms also sell paid plans; where a card says a sign-up is free, that is the agency's or platform's own statement.

The platforms, in one place

OpenGov

DeSoto, Sarasota, Pinellas and Collier counties; Cape Coral and Fort Myers. Free vendor account (Sarasota, Cape Coral and Pinellas pages say so); subscribe to each agency's portal to get its notices. Sarasota says no login is needed to search and view.

DemandStar (Euna OpenBids)

Hardee and Hendry counties, Arcadia, Wauchula, Manatee (alongside its own RFP Post), SWFWMD and LCEC. Manatee County says registration is free for its bids and notices; check DemandStar for other agencies.

BidNet Direct (Florida Purchasing Group)

Highlands and Okeechobee counties and Sebring. BidNet says the free "Limited Access" registration sends email notices for matching bids.

IonWave, Euna/Bonfire and county sites

Lee County and Punta Gorda use IonWave; Hillsborough uses the Euna Procurement (Bonfire) platform. Charlotte (Purchasing Bids Online), Polk and Glades post on their own websites; Glades has asked for sealed paper responses.

A weekly routine

  • Register once on each platform your counties use (above) and pick commodity codes for directional boring, utility construction and water/sewer lines, so notices come to you.
  • Read the plan or proposal holders list on big jobs (FDOT publishes one for every letting proposal). Those primes may need a bore sub: post on the sub board.
  • Note pre-bid meetings (some are mandatory) and check for addenda before you submit.
  • FDOT: to bid an FDOT construction contract over $250,000 as the prime, a contractor must first be certified (prequalified) by FDOT (F.S. 337.14(1), checked Nov 28, 2026). Many bore crews work FDOT jobs as subs; see the FDOT Section 555 spec desk.
  • Before you price it: FDOT price benchmarks, ROW permit cheat sheets, job site check and the licensing page (Florida underground utility work needs the right license; Bore411 never lends or shares one).
