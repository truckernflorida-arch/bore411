Rig sizes

Which rig fits the job?

A quick guide to HDD rig size classes using published manufacturer specs, plus a rough rule-of-thumb picker. Use it to screen bids, not to engineer a bore.

Quick picker

Suggested class

—

Rough estimate, confirm with your drilling engineer. Soil, rock, bore path, bundle size and the pipe maker's pull limits decide the real answer.

Assumptions (rules of thumb, not sourced specs): up to 4 in under 1,000 LF → small; 4 in over 1,000 LF, 6 in, or 8–12 in up to 1,500 LF → mid; 8–12 in over 1,500 LF, or 14 in and up → large; major waterway crossings → maxi. No source we found gives a pullback number per pipe size, so these lean conservative. The same rules tag each lead on the leads list as “est.”, using the pipe sizes and footage in the posting text; leads without a size show “Check specs.”

What spec sheets don't tell you: manufacturers don't publish a maximum bore length or diameter. The sheets list a minimum bore diameter (4 in) and call backream diameter “soil dependent.” Real capability depends on soil and rock, bore path, mud program and crew. Florida limestone and karst can change everything.

Typical use (rule of thumb)

Small–mid (about 20k–40k lb)

JT20, JT30, D24x40, D40x55, JT40. Fiber, conduit and small water road crossings. Most utility work in Florida sits here.

Mid–large (40k–100k+ lb)

Larger force-main or water-main crossings, roughly 8–16 in HDPE and long shots.

Maxi (about 220k lb)

Big river or intracoastal crossings.

Estimates, not specs

These ranges are our reading of the specs above and common industry practice. We found no published source tying pipe size to a required pullback.

Support-equipment checklist

  • Locator / tracking electronics
  • Mud mixing system and tank (for example, one used D24x40 package came with a 1,000-gallon mixing system)
  • Vac truck / vacuum system
  • Reamers and pulling heads sized to the product pipe
  • Mud recycling and mud pumps for large and maxi rigs
  • Truck and trailer to move the package
