Right-of-way permit cheat sheets

Who issues the permit to bore in public right-of-way in the fourteen counties around Arcadia, and what their own pages say. Every item links to the official county or FDOT source. Checked November 1, 2026; Hillsborough, Pinellas, Collier and Okeechobee added November 21, 2026.

Informational only. Not legal advice. Rules, fees and forms change; confirm with the permitting office before you bid or start work. A right-of-way permit does not replace your Sunshine 811 ticket (F.S. 556) or any license the work requires.

First: who owns the road?

State road (FDOT)

Twelve of the counties below are in FDOT District One; Hillsborough and Pinellas are in FDOT District Seven. Either way, utility work on state-road right-of-way is permitted by FDOT through One Stop Permitting (OSP), not by the county.

Sources: FDOT Utilities Office · FDOT District One utilities · District One county map · District Seven county map.

County road or city street

County-maintained roads go through the county office listed below. Streets inside a city (for example Arcadia, Punta Gorda, Sebring, Wauchula, Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Tampa, Clearwater, Naples) are usually permitted by the city, so check with the city when the job is inside city limits. One bore can cross more than one owner, which can mean more than one permit.

DeSoto County

Permit: Right-of-Way Permit from DeSoto County Engineering, for work that encroaches on county-maintained roads.

  • Fill in the Right-of-Way Permit Application (PDF) and email it to Engineering (address on the county forms page).
  • The form asks for the work address, crossroads, proposed work and, for new construction, the building permit number.
  • The applicant is responsible for utility locates (F.S. 556); traffic must be maintained per the MUTCD.
  • An approved permit is good for 6 months or until the project is done, whichever comes first; a 30-day extension can be requested.
  • Fee: shown as a blank on the form; ask Engineering.

Sources: DeSoto permit applications & forms · ROW permit application (PDF) · Engineering

Charlotte County

Permit: Right of Way Utility Permit, required for all utility installations, overhead or underground, in county right-of-way and easements (Ordinance 2021-009 and the Engineering Design & Construction Standards).

  • Applied for in Accela with the subcontractor worksheet, construction plans and MOT (traffic) plans; Public Works and Charlotte County Utilities review it.
  • Contractors must register with the county (licenses and insurance) to work in the right-of-way. A bond is required in advance for utility contractors (home services excepted).
  • Fees: $140 right-of-way review at application, plus an issuance fee set by Public Works by scope. The county reports it does not collect permit fees on fiber permits because of the Communications Services Tax.
  • Before boring: locates at least 48 hours ahead, written notice to county staff 48 hours before work, daily street sheets, and potholing to physically locate utilities.

Sources: ROW Utility Permit · ROW permit requirements · County presentation, May 19, 2026 (PDF)

Hardee County

Office: Hardee County Public Works (Wauchula).

  • We did not find a published right-of-way or utility permit form online. Contact Public Works before boring in county right-of-way.
  • Utility design and easement rules are in Section 5.10 of the Unified Land Development Code (adopted October 12, 2023).
  • Fee and timeline: data being compiled.

Sources: Public Works · Unified Land Development Code (PDF)

Highlands County

Permit: General Utility Permit from County Engineering, for all underground and overhead utility crossings on and along county roads and public right-of-way.

  • Apply online through eTRAKiT with the checklist items, including scaled installation drawings.
  • Fee: $400 for a General Utility Permit; unauthorized work in the right-of-way is $1,000 plus restoration (county fee schedule, checked Nov 1, 2026).

Sources: Utility Permits · Online submittal checklist (PDF) · Fee schedule · eTRAKiT

Sarasota County

Permit: Right-of-Way Use Permit (Public Works), applied for online in Accela under the Public Works module.

  • Application requirements: data being compiled (Sarasota County's site blocked our automated check; open the Accela link directly).
  • Work on county water and sewer systems also needs a separate Utility Construction Permit from Public Utilities, which asks for copies of any right-of-way use permits.
  • Fee: data being compiled.

Sources: Online permitting (Accela) · Public Utilities permitting

Manatee County

Permit: Right-of-Way Use Permit, required before installing utilities, fiber, gas lines, culverts or other facilities in public right-of-way.

  • Telephone, electric, cable TV, gas and other private-sector operations must apply. The county says it processes about 400 of these permits a year.
  • Applied for through the county's online services portal.
  • Fee: data being compiled.

Source: Request a Right of Way Use Permit

Lee County

Office: Lee County DOT, Engineering Services, issues permits for construction in county right-of-way.

  • Use the DOT right-of-way application and the checklist for utility and communication providers.
  • Details and fees: data being compiled (Lee County's site blocked our automated check; open the links directly).

Sources: Lee DOT Engineering Services · ROW application (PDF) · Utility & communication provider checklist (PDF)

Glades County

Office: Glades County Road & Bridge Department (Moore Haven), which issues permits for utilities in county-owned right-of-way.

  • Road rules are in Chapter 58 (Roads and Bridges) of the county code.
  • Form, fee and timeline: data being compiled; contact Road & Bridge.

Sources: Road & Bridge Department · Code Chapter 58

Hendry County

Permit: Utility Permit from the County Engineer (LaBelle), with a sketch or set of plans attached.

  • Underground crossings: at least 30 inches below pavement and 18 inches below the roadside ditch, unless staff approve an exception.
  • If jack and bore is permitted, the bore must extend at least 5 feet past the edge of pavement.
  • Inspection requests by email only, 24 hours before the work. Survey and mark the right-of-way lines before starting.
  • Disturbed right-of-way must be grassed or seeded after the work.
  • Fee: data being compiled.

Source: Hendry County Utilities Permit and conditions

Polk County

Office: Polk County Roads and Drainage reviews Utility Right of Way Permits and road and lane closures on county roads.

  • Lane closure request needed when working within 15 feet of the edge of a county road for more than two hours.
  • Submit closure requests at least 10 working days ahead, with a location map and a Temporary Traffic Control (TTC) plan. No fee for a closure request.
  • Utility permit fee: data being compiled.

Source: Polk County Roads and Drainage

Hillsborough County

Permit: ROW Use Permit – Other Uses from Development Services (Right-of-Way Permitting), applied for in the HillsGovHub portal. Unincorporated county only: the county does not issue permits inside Tampa, Plant City or Temple Terrace.

  • Fees: $50 base application fee per permit, plus $150 for open trench or trenchless work parallel to the road, $100 for a trenchless road crossing of 2 in diameter or less, $200 over 2 in, and a $300 special activity fee for work that needs full-time inspection (the schedule lists directional drilling and jack and bore under major roads, water bodies and structures). Fee schedule dated 8/2020; fees are due on approval.
  • Minimum cover for trenched and trenchless installs: 36 in below roadway surfaces and 30 in below existing ground (communication lines may have 24 in where not under pavement); 18 in vertical clearance from other facilities; 30 in below the design ditch bottom; normally no deeper than 48 in of cover (Utility Accommodation Guide 5.4.4).
  • Where marks cross or overlap the drill path, expose the utility by hand digging or vacuum excavation; driveway crossings must be bored, not cut; roadway crossings or work under pavement less than five years old must be jack and bore or HDD (Guide 5.4.1, 5.4.9, 4.7).
  • A separate Temporary Traffic Control permit is needed when traffic is impeded or for work within 15 ft of the edge of the road lasting more than two hours.

Sources: Apply for a ROW permit · ROW permit fee schedule (PDF) · Utility Accommodation Guide & ROW Use Procedures, Oct 2023 (PDF). Checked Nov 21, 2026.

Pinellas County

Permit: Right-of-Way Utilization Permit (or an annual general permit for routine work), required before placing facilities in county right-of-way and for any excavation or lane, parking or sidewalk closure there (County Code Sec. 154-302). The utility work article covers all county rights-of-way, including inside city limits.

  • Apply in the Pinellas County Access Portal (Development › Create an Application › Right of Way) with the utilization permit application and a survey or plan of the work.
  • The application must include scaled, dated drawings with known existing facilities, a maintenance of traffic plan (MUTCD / FDOT 600 series), the construction method, a schedule, and for underground work the location, depth, size, material and quantity of new conduit; some projects need Florida PE-certified plans.
  • Sureties: completion surety of 110% of the engineer's estimated cost and a maintenance surety of at least 20% of that for 18 months; the code exempts some providers. Proof of insurance before approval.
  • Fee: data being compiled (the application says a variable processing fee is due before the permit issues).

Sources: Apply for a Utilization Permit · Utilization permit application (PDF) · County Code Ch. 154, Art. VI (Utility Work). Checked Nov 21, 2026.

Collier County

Permit: Right-of-Way Permit (Commercial and Franchise Utility), applied for in the county's GMCD portal (cvportal.collier.gov). Needed for work in public right-of-way in unincorporated Collier and on county-maintained roads inside cities.

  • Since April 1, 2026, commercial or utility work that includes jack and bore, open cut or other excavation in the right-of-way needs a performance bond (Ordinance 2026-06); the county posts single-job and annual blanket bond forms.
  • Plans must show offsets from centerline, pavement and right-of-way, and all existing improvements within 100 ft; a directional bore must be labeled with its length, size and depth. Pneumatic piercing tools are not approved (ROW Handbook).
  • The county's Roadway ROW Survey lists which roads need a county ROW permit; lane closures use the county's notification form and MOT policy.
  • Fee: the county posts a ROW permit fee schedule last updated 2017; amounts are data being compiled.

Sources: Right-of-Way Permitting · ROW Permitting & Inspection Handbook (PDF). Collier's site blocked parts of our automated check; open the links directly. Checked Nov 21, 2026.

Okeechobee County

Permit: Permit for Utility Construction in Public Right-of-Way, issued by the county administrator or designee (board-approved) before any surface, underground or overhead utility goes in county right-of-way (County Code Sec. 58-34). Public Works (Road & Bridge Division) maintains the county roads.

  • Attach at least two sketches or drawings showing the centerline of the install relative to roads, pavement and ditches, plus a cross section. Notify other right-of-way users nearby by certified mail; they have seven working days to object.
  • Fee in the code: $250 for utility permits ($75 driveways); the board can change it by resolution.
  • Installation standards (Sec. 58-35): cross under pavement without cutting it where possible (jacked casing at least 4 ft past each pavement edge; mechanical boring allowed); minimum 36 in cover over pipe, and for direct-burial cable 36 in under roadways and 30 in elsewhere in the right-of-way; restore sod in kind.
  • Security: a county development document says a surety bond, letter of credit or other security is required for any work in county right-of-way and must be kept through a one-year warranty period after completion; a maintenance of traffic plan as needed. Timeline: data being compiled.

Sources: Okeechobee County Code, Ch. 58 (Municode) · Public Works · County document on right-of-way security (PDF). Code text read from a public copy because Municode blocked our automated check; confirm the current sections. Checked Nov 21, 2026.
