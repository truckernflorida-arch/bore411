FDOT directional bore spec desk (Section 555)

What FDOT's directional bore spec asks of the drilling crew, start to finish, with the deadlines worked out and a Bore Path Report draft you can fill in from your rod log. Built for HDD crews bidding or subbing FDOT conduit work around Arcadia. Informational only — not legal advice, not a substitute for the contract.

Which rules apply? Section 555 is part of the FDOT Standard Specifications for Road and Bridge Construction, FY 2026-27 edition (effective July 1, 2026; Section 555 on pages 795–799). It governs FDOT construction contracts, and Section 630 says conduit installed by directional bore follows it (630-3.1). County, city and utility jobs only follow it if their contract or permit says so. Your contract, plans, special provisions and the Engineer govern. We compared Section 555 in the FY 2027-28 edition (effective July 1, 2027): the text is unchanged.

Deadlines

"Within seven days" (555-5.1) is counted here as calendar days; FDOT 1-3 defines a calendar day as every day on the calendar, but 555-5.1 does not say which kind of day, so confirm with the Engineer. If product damage is suspected: stop, notify the Engineer, pressure test within 24 hours if required; the Engineer has up to 72 hours to rule (555-3.1.1).

3. Pay length check (Section 630)

FDOT pays underground conduit per foot of horizontal straight-line length between centers of pull boxes, cabinets, poles, etc., regardless of the length or number of conduits, with no allowance for sweeps or depth (630-4). The unit price includes the boring, casings, spoils, drilling fluid disposal, locate wire, flowable fill and restoration (630-4). Under existing pavement (roadway, driveways or sidewalk) it is paid as directional bore unless identified as open trench; if one pull-box-to-pull-box run is under both turf and pavement, the whole run is paid as directional bore (630-5).

Result

Estimate; the plans and the Engineer's measurement govern. Compare your price per paid foot with FDOT averages on the bid sheet. Conduit material rules (for example HDPE SDR 11 or lower for ITS and signal conduit in earth, SDR 13.5 or lower for lighting) are in 630-3.1; a run may not have more than 270° of bends between terminations without a pull box (630-3.1).

4. Joint test check (555-3.1.2)

Where the product is under any pavement (including sidewalk) and front shoulders, FDOT requires a water tight pipe and joint configuration: no leakage at a sustained 5 psi. Elsewhere it must meet soil tight requirements: up to 0.1 gallon of leakage at a sustained 2 psi. Joint integrity tests run for one hour. The Engineer decides where water tight applies, including for future widening.

Against the spec numbers

Compares your numbers with 555-3.1.2 only. Also follow the product maker's pressure test recommendations and any owner pressure requirement for carrier pipe. The Engineer reviews and approves results.

5. Bore Path Report builder (555-5.1)

FDOT wants a Bore Path Report within seven days of finishing each bore path, and no payment for the pay item until it is accepted (555-6). This builds a plain-text draft with the five required items. It stays in this browser; nothing is sent to Bore411.

5. Elevation and offset points

Per 555-3.2(8): elevations on top of the product and offsets from the product centerline to the fixed feature, at every vertical and horizontal alignment correction, enough to plot the alignment, at least three points.
