## 2026-11-20
- Bid pages: new "Geology & rock (FGS)" card at the point picked in the soil card (town/county center or typed job-site coordinates), queried live in the browser from Florida Geological Survey layers on FDEP open data (free, no key; checked Nov 20, 2026). It answers "will I hit rock?" before you price the footage:
  - Geologic unit at or near the surface from FGS STATEMAP Geology (1:24,000; FGS maps the first recognizable unit within 20 ft of the surface), with lithology, age and FGS's own unit description; falls back to the FGS statewide Stratigraphic Geology map where STATEMAP has no polygon.
  - Near-surface rock or sediment class from the FGS Environmental Geology map (published 2001), the sinkhole type area from Sinkhole Types (Sinclair & Stewart, 1985, FGS Map Series 110), and the FGS sinkhole favorability model class (Least favorable / Favorable / More favorable / Most favorable).
  - FGS core and cuttings repository wells within 3 miles: count, how many have a lithologic or driller's log on file, and the 5 nearest (with a log when available) with W-number, distance, total depth, year drilled and logs on file, each linked to the public FGS GeoDES borehole summary. Well names, owners and drillers are not requested or shown; confidential records are skipped. Notes FGS's statement that about 60% of well locations have been verified.
  - When the mapped lithology or near-surface class includes limestone, dolostone/dolomite or chert, the card says so and suggests asking for borings or a geotechnical report and pricing possible rock tooling before locking in a per-foot price (labeled a Bore411 reading, not an FGS finding). Where FGS maps undifferentiated sediments (more than 20 ft of cover), it notes a shallow bore may stay in sand and clay. Labeled screening-level, not a geotechnical report.
- County pages (`county/*.html`) show the same card after the road owner card (added by `county.js`, no regeneration). Home page bid-desk list mentions it.
- Files: `common.js` (`geoAt`, `geoHtml`, `favAt`), `bid.html`, `county.js`, `index.html`, `README.md`, `sitemap.xml`. No forms, analytics, CNAME or `leads.json` changed.

## 2026-11-11
- Bore calculators (`calc.html#log`): new card 6, "Rod-by-rod bore log (as-built record)". Enter the entry pitch and rod length, then the locator's pitch at the end of each rod (plus its depth reading and a note if you want). It works out how far out and how deep below entry grade the head is after every rod with the average angle method (each rod uses the mean of its start and end pitch angles; ISCWSA "Introduction to Wellbore Positioning" 7.1, checked Nov 11, 2026), shows the deepest point, and highlights any rod where the locator depth and the calculated depth differ by a foot or more. Works with locators that show downhill pitch as minus or plus. The log stays in the browser (localStorage, not sent to Bore411) until cleared; Undo last rod, Copy log (tab-separated, pastes into a spreadsheet) and Download CSV. Labeled an estimate and a field record, not a surveyed as-built; left/right steering is not tracked.

## 2026-11-07
- Bore calculators (`calc.html`): new card 4, "Pullback force estimate (mini-HDD)". Pick IPS size, SDR 9–17, number of conduits, horizontal pull length, planned 90° bends and drill rod diameter; it estimates the pull on the conduit at the end of the pull with the PPI Handbook of PE Pipe, Chapter 12 (revised May 2026) mini-HDD method, Eqs. 22–25: F = w_b × L/3 × 1.6^n, w_b = OD²/2 − pipe weight, unplanned path corrections = 2L/(500 × rod diameter). Pipe weight uses minimum wall and 0.947 g/cm³ (low end of the ASTM D3350 PE4710 density cell). It compares the result per conduit with the TN-63 1-hour safe pull already on the page and flags when the estimate is over the standard-strength limit. Formula, source and Nov 7, 2026 check date added to "Formulas and sources". Labeled an estimate; not for maxi-HDD (ASTM F1962).

## 2026-10-30
- Bid pages: the "Sinkhole screening" card is now live (was "coming soon"). It uses the point picked in the soil card (town/county center or job-site coordinates) and queries the Florida Geological Survey Subsidence Incident Reports database (FDEP open data) live in the browser: reported incidents within 1, 3, 5 and 10 miles, how many are field-verified sinkholes, and the 5 nearest reports with FGS reference number, distance, reported date, verified status and size. Shows the statewide report count and newest event date from the live database. Labeled screening-level, not a geotechnical report; notes that most reports are not field-checked and that no reports is not a clearance. Owner and reporter details are not requested or shown.
