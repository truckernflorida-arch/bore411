Florida underground utility license checker

For crews thinking about the state-certified Underground Utility and Excavation Contractor license (DBPR license prefix CU; county-registered is RU). Built from the Florida Statutes and the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) rules. Checked November 3, 2026.

Informational only. Not legal advice and not a DBPR decision. The CILB decides eligibility from your actual application and proof of experience. Bore411 does not hold, lend or share contractor licenses; letting someone else work under your license is a disciplinary violation (F.S. 489.129(1)(e)).

1. Do I meet an experience path?

Florida's paths for certification by exam are in F.S. 489.111(2)(c). A full year means at least 2,000 hours of work.

2. Credit, insurance and exams

What the CILB rules ask for after you meet an experience path.

  • Insurance: public liability of at least $100,000 and property damage of at least $25,000 for the underground utility and excavation category (Rule 61G4-15.003, F.A.C.).
  • Exams: the Construction Business and Finance exam and the trade knowledge exam. The exam application (CILB 1) is approved by Professional Testing, Inc., and computer-based exams are scheduled with Pearson VUE (DBPR exam information). With a Building Construction degree (or a related degree the board approves by rule) and a 3.0 GPA, only the business and finance portion is required (F.S. 489.113(1)).
  • Fees and forms: see CILB applications and forms. Fee amounts: data being compiled.

What the license covers for HDD crews

From the definition in F.S. 489.105(3)(n), the subcontracting rules in F.S. 489.113(3), and the exemptions in F.S. 489.103 and F.S. 489.503.

Working as a sub

You need your own license for this category

An unlicensed sub may work under a licensed contractor's supervision only for work that would not itself need a specialty license in the categories of 489.105(3)(d)–(o). Underground utility and excavation is (n), so a sub doing that work needs its own certification or registration (F.S. 489.113(2)).

Unlicensed contracts and license lending

A contract signed by an unlicensed contractor can't be enforced by that contractor (F.S. 489.128). A license holder who lets an unlicensed person or business use the license, or assists unlicensed contracting, can be fined up to $10,000 per violation, suspended or revoked (F.S. 489.129(1)(d)–(e)).

Certified (CU) vs. registered (RU)

Certified is statewide after the state exam (F.S. 489.113(1)). Registered works only in the cities and counties where you met the local licensing rules, and only for the work the registration covers (F.S. 489.117(1)(b)).

Verify a license before you sign
